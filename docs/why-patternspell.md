# Why PatternSpell Exists

## What it is

PatternSpell is a tool for elementary teachers to build spelling lists organized by phonics pattern and display them as a clear chart on a projector or smartboard. One column per spelling pattern, all words visible at once.

## Who it's for

Elementary teachers and homeschool educators who teach spelling through phonics patterns. It works with any curriculum: CKLA, Fundations, UFLI, Words Their Way, or a teacher's own sequence. If you teach spelling by pattern, this tool is for you. If your spelling program is pure memorization of unrelated word lists, it is not.

## The problem it solves

Two things every pattern-based teacher does are slow and fiddly by hand:

1. **Building the list.** Picking words that actually follow the week's patterns, grouping them correctly, and getting the groupings right takes real time every week.
2. **Displaying it.** Getting a whole class to see the words clearly on a board, grouped so the pattern jumps out, is hard with handwriting, printed sheets, or generic slides.

PatternSpell does both: build the list once, show it as a legible chart on any display.

## Why patterns

One pattern learned is dozens of words spelled. When students see words grouped by shared spelling pattern, they learn the underlying rule and can spell new words they have never seen. Memorizing a list teaches only the listed words; research on spelling interventions shows memorization-only lessons show no transfer to general spelling ability, while pattern instruction does.

This is not a fringe view. Every major phonics program organizes spelling around patterns: Words Their Way (word sorts), Fundations (one pattern per week), CKLA (spelling alternatives charts), UFLI (sound-to-spelling routines). PatternSpell is the display and list-building layer that sits on top of whatever pattern sequence a teacher already follows.

## Why a chart, not a tree

CKLA's official display is a "spelling tree" with words as leaves on branches. Classroom teachers consistently replace it with column-based pattern charts: the leaves are hard to cut out and hard for kids to see from their seats. A verified teacher review of a popular chart replacement: "I prefer this product much more to any I have seen using trees."

PatternSpell's chart (one column per pattern, all words visible at once, ordered by frequency) is the display teachers choose when given the choice. It is the market-validated format, not an invention.

## How teachers use it day to day

1. Pick the spelling patterns for the week.
2. Add words to each pattern.
3. Display the chart during the lesson on a projector or smartboard.
4. Share the list with other teachers (a simple share/receive flow, no technical jargon).
5. Print or export for student practice.

## What it is not

- Not a game site.
- Not a replacement for a phonics curriculum.
- Not a tool for memorizing random word lists.
- Not a student-facing app (teachers build and display; students practice from what the teacher shows).
