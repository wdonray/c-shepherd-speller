# PatternSpell Research Summary: Why Pattern-Based Spelling Tools Are Useful

Researched October 10, 2026. Purpose: validate whether PatternSpell (a teacher tool for building spelling lists organized by phonics/spelling pattern, displayed as a pattern chart) is broadly useful or too specific. This document is the evidence base for PatternSpell's positioning, landing page copy, and user guides.

## The core argument in one paragraph

Every major phonics program teaches spelling as patterns, not as random word lists. Words Their Way, Fundations, CKLA, and UFLI all organize instruction around recurring letter-sound correspondences (word families, spelling alternatives, sorts). Teachers consistently report that when students see words grouped by shared pattern, they learn the underlying rule and can spell new words they have never seen, instead of memorizing each word in isolation. PatternSpell exists to make that grouping fast to build and easy to display on a projector or smartboard.

## 1. Why patterns beat memorized lists (the practitioner case)

**Fundations (Wilson Language Training).** A teacher on ProTeacher Community, describing years of Fundations use across K-3, put the program's philosophy plainly: a traditional spelling list does not work because students memorize words instead of learning the "why" behind how words work. In Fundations, each week teaches one phonics skill, pattern, or spelling rule, and practice words are chosen because they follow that pattern. She stopped sending home weekly spelling lists entirely and sent the phonics focus instead; every student achieved mastery.
Source: https://proteacher.net/discussions/threads/fundations.561199/

**Science of Reading / orthographic mapping.** Lexia's guide to orthographic mapping lists "word sorting by shared spelling patterns" as a core effective activity, and frames the alternative this way: rather than asking students to memorize how a word looks, pattern work asks them to analyze how a word works, segmenting by sound and linking each sound to its letters.
Source: https://WWW.LEXIALEARNING.COM/blog/orthographic-mapping-connections-lead-to-literacy-success

**EdWeek on spelling research (2025).** Researcher Shane Chandler, discussing a meta-analysis of spelling interventions, said the goal is not memorization: "We don't want kids to just memorize words. We want them to unlock the pattern." Lessons aimed at memorizing whole words improved only the specific memorized words, with no transfer to general spelling ability.
Source: https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/why-teaching-spelling-can-boost-students-reading-skills/2025/10

**K12Reader (practitioner guide).** "Teaching children these basic patterns will help them master the spelling of many words," using the example that learning one vowel pattern unlocks a whole family of words. Direct pattern instruction is presented as the mechanism that turns a dozen memorized words into hundreds of spellable ones.
Source: https://www.k12reader.com/how-spelling-instruction-relates-to-reading-ability/

**Homeschool practitioner (Well-Trained Mind forums).** A parent comparing methods noted the arithmetic that sells patterns: with sight-word memorization a child must memorize each word separately, "but with phonics, they only have to memorize about 70 phonograms and 28 rules." Her daughter could read at a 4th-grade level from memorized words yet could not read an unfamiliar word until she learned the patterns.
Source: https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/483163-is-a-sight-words-approach-more-classical/

**SpellingJoy (practitioner blog).** Recommends integrating high-frequency words into phonics lessons by pattern (teaching "sh" with she/should/show), arguing this "prevents sight words and phonics lessons from being two separate, disconnected activities."
Source: https://spellingjoy.com/blog/sight-words-vs-phonics-teaching-both

## 2. Trees are confusing; charts are not (validates PatternSpell's display decision)

This is the most directly relevant finding for PatternSpell's design. CKLA's official display is a "spelling tree" (words hung on branches as leaves). Teachers on Teachers Pay Teachers report actively replacing it with column-based pattern charts.

**TPT seller "Give me a 2nd" (CKLA 2nd Grade code chart / spelling tree alternative, 5.0 rating).** The seller, a CKLA classroom teacher, wrote that after her first year of CKLA, the trees "drove me crazy": cutting out leaves and ducks for the spelling trees was a pain, and "the kids couldn't see those little leaves very well when they went back to their seats." Her replacement is a columnar chart with sounds, spelling patterns, and power bars that "can support or even replace the class spelling trees." A verified buyer (leah e., 2nd grade teacher) reviewed: "I prefer this product much more to any I have seen using trees. I highly recommend this thoughtful product."
Source: https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/CKLA-2nd-Grade-Code-chart-spelling-tree-alternative-11795960

**Why this matters for PatternSpell.** Donray's locked decision (pattern chart showing all words at once, one column per pattern, ordered by frequency with a power bar) matches exactly what working teachers build for themselves when the official materials fail them. The chart is not a novel invention; it is the teacher-preferred display that the market already validates with purchases and 5-star reviews. The tree can return later as an optional view, but the chart is the default teachers actually use.

## 3. How the major programs position pattern work (who it's for)

**Words Their Way (Pearson).** Positioned as a developmental spelling program for K-8: students are assessed, placed at a spelling stage, and work with word sorts at their level. A 2017 study in the Journal of Literacy Research found statistically significant spelling improvement versus traditional memorization over a semester. The program's own materials emphasize that sorts double as formative assessment: teachers watch students sort and give immediate feedback. Enormous teacher ecosystem on TPT (word-family bundles with 500+ ratings).
Source: https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/Resources/TvqxML/5OK104/words_their-way-word-sorts.pdf

**Fundations (Wilson).** Positioned as a prevention program for K-3 general education classrooms to "reduce reading and spelling failure." Highly scripted, multisensory, cumulative. Teachers teach one pattern per week; spelling is never a disconnected list.
Source: https://www.wilsonlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Fundations-Program-Overview-Levels-K-3_V5.pdf

**CKLA (Amplify).** Positioned as a full ELA curriculum. Its "Spelling Alternatives" lessons introduce each sound's multiple spellings systematically (e.g., /ee/ spelled ee, ea, e_e, ey, y), and its charts show all alternatives at once. This is the closest official precedent for PatternSpell's display: one sound, every spelling, all visible simultaneously.
Source: https://dam.amplify.com/m/4a73c7c700d5ca25/original/CKLA_G1_U07_TG_3E-UVL.pdf

**UFLI (University of Florida Literacy Institute).** The Science-of-Reading-aligned routine now widely adopted: consistent multisensory sequence (say it, tap it, map it, write it) that connects each sound to its spellings. TPT sellers package UFLI-aligned spelling routines as some of their best-reviewed products.
Source: https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/UFLI-Aligned-Spelling-Routine-Science-of-Reading-Multisensory-Literacy-16973092

## 4. How spelling tools position themselves (market language)

**VocabularySpellingCity (founded 2008).** Slogan: "Build Word Skills While Playing Games: For Districts, Schools, Teachers, and Parents." Core pitch: teachers create custom lists, students practice through 35+ games. An independent efficacy study is cited: 43% increase in vocabulary retention, 21.5% reading comprehension gain for native speakers. Notably, their phonics patent page says the sound-to-spelling work came directly from teacher requests: teachers "asked us to do more with helping students with sounds," wanting students to "connect the sounds that they hear and the letter combinations that they see."
Source: https://bouncewatch.com/company/vocabularyspellingcity

**TPT word-family sellers.** The highest-rated products (4.8-4.9 stars, hundreds of reviews) all promise the same things: Science of Reading alignment, no-prep usability, small-group and center use, and editable lists so teachers add their own words. Representative review: "These have been extremely helpful in understanding CVC words and decoding sounds." The market signal is consistent: teachers buy pattern-organized, classroom-ready, customizable materials, and they buy a lot of them.

## 5. Is it too specific? (addressing the one-curriculum concern)

Chaley used PatternSpell with one curriculum. The honest question is whether the tool generalizes. The research says yes, for three reasons:

1. **The underlying task is curriculum-agnostic.** Every program above (Words Their Way, Fundations, CKLA, UFLI) requires teachers to present words grouped by pattern and to build custom lists at the right level. A tool that builds pattern-organized lists and displays them as a chart serves all of them; nothing in the concept is CKLA-specific.
2. **The pain it removes is universal.** Teachers in every program report the same friction: building pattern-grouped lists by hand is slow, and displaying them legibly on a board is hard. TPT's thriving market for pre-made pattern materials (hundreds of 5-star reviews) proves teachers will adopt anything that removes this friction.
3. **The display matches the winning format.** The pattern chart is what teachers choose when given the choice (see section 2). PatternSpell's locked display is the market-validated one, not an idiosyncratic invention.

The genuine specificity risk is narrower: PatternSpell is for teachers who teach spelling through patterns at all. Teachers running pure memorization-based weekly lists (the approach every source above argues against) are not the audience. That is a positioning decision, not a flaw: the tool is for pattern-based spelling instruction, which is also the direction the Science of Reading consensus points.

## 6. Positioning recommendations for PatternSpell

Based on the above, the landing page and guides should say, in plain teacher language:

- **Who it's for:** Elementary teachers (and homeschool educators) who teach spelling through phonics patterns, in any curriculum: CKLA, Fundations, UFLI, Words Their Way, or their own sequence.
- **What problem it solves:** Building pattern-organized spelling lists by hand is slow, and displaying them so a whole class can see is hard. PatternSpell builds the list once and shows it as a clear chart on any projector or smartboard.
- **How teachers use it day-to-day:** Pick the patterns for the week, add words, display the chart during the lesson, print or share the list for practice. Lists can be shared with other teachers (import/export framed as sharing, not technical file transfer).
- **Why patterns:** One pattern learned is dozens of words spelled. Memorizing lists teaches only the listed words; patterns transfer to words students have never seen.
- **What it is not:** Not a game site, not a replacement for a phonics curriculum, not for memorizing random word lists. It is the display and list-building layer that sits on top of whatever pattern sequence the teacher already follows.
