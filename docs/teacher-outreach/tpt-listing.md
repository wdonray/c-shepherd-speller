## TpT rules that matter

- The first resource a seller lists must be free. This list is
  free, so it fits.
- Resources must be your original authorship, complete, and
  accurate. This list was drafted from the app's own UFLI
  Grade 2 Week 7 list (Lesson 84). Donray should read it, edit
  it in his own voice, and list it as his own work only if he
  is comfortable doing so.
- A resource must be accessible directly from TpT. This PDF is
  the resource: a complete one-page word list with a lesson.
  The link to patternspell.org is a supplement, not a gate.
  The tool is free with no login, no ads, and no student
  accounts, so nothing else is required to use it.
- Do not link buyers to another sales channel. PatternSpell is
  not a sales channel. It is free, with nothing for sale.
- TpT says the majority of resources in a store should be paid.
  A single free list is a starting point, not a store plan.

## Listing copy

Title: ai and ay Spelling Words - UFLI Lesson 84 Grade 2 - Free Printable Word List

Price: Free

Grades: 2nd. Also works for grades 1-3.

Subjects: English Language Arts, Spelling

Resource type: Activities, Worksheets

Description:

A free printable ai and ay word list for Grade 2, built around
PatternSpell, a free chart tool at
https://patternspell.org/word-lists/ufli-lesson-84-ai-ay.

ai and ay both spell the long a sound. ai sits in the middle of
a word, ay sits at the end. The chart shows one column per
pattern, all the words at once, so the class can read it
together on a projector or smartboard.

What is inside:

- All 12 words for UFLI Lesson 84, Grade 2 Week 7: rain,
  train, wait, snail, paint, chain, play, day, say, away,
  stay, tray
- A 10-minute lesson plan, no printing required
- Steps for showing the chart, listening for the pattern,
  sorting, spelling, and using the words in sentences
- A dictation list and sentence work to try

The tool is free, with no login, no ads, and no student
accounts. It works on projectors, smartboards, tablets, and
Chromebooks.

If you try it, the builder would love to hear what happened.
Email donrayxwilliams@gmail.com with the grade, what worked,
and what confused students.
